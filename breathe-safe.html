Breathe Safe

Making everyday physical activity safer and more understandable for people living with chronic asthma

About the project

Breathe Safe is an app that replaces guesswork with real-time guidance for chronic asthma patients, helping them read their body's own signals instead of relying on gut-feeling during physical activity. It connects patients, caregivers, and doctors through breathing status tracking, activity logging, and an immediate caregiver alert system.

Overview

Problem

Chronic asthma patients have no reliable way to know their real-time physical limits during activity, because:

  1. No objective signal for real-time limits — patients rely on gut sense alone, not data.
  2. Caregivers only find out something is wrong after symptoms are already visible.
  3. Doctors have limited visibility into patients' real-world activity patterns between visits.
  4. Physiotherapist guidance doesn't extend to unsupervised daily exertion at home.

Strengths and Weaknesses

Strengths
  • Only app connecting caregivers in real time — none of the 7 competitors have a Notify Caregiver flow
  • Combines asthma-specific tracking with real-time feedback — a pairing no competitor offers
  • Built directly from real interview findings, not generic health tracking
Weaknesses
  • New entrant with no existing user base or clinical validation, unlike established players like Propeller Health
  • No environmental/weather trigger tracking yet — an area Propeller Health partially covers
Gap
  • Established players (Propeller Health, Apple Health) have far larger user bases and could add caregiver features of their own
  • General fitness apps already have real-time feedback infrastructure and could bolt on asthma-specific modules
Opportunity
  • Add environmental/weather trigger alerts — a gap no competitor fully solves
  • Partner with doctors/physiotherapists for clinical validation
  • Expand caregiver features further, since this is completely unaddressed market-wide

Primary Research

Primary Research

Research Objective

To understand how chronic asthma patients recognize and respond to their physical limits during daily and heavy activity, what role caregivers, doctors, and physiotherapists play in that process, and how confident patients feel managing exertion without any real-time support.

Research Methodology

  • Method: Semi-structured interviews (in-person, qualitative) + online survey (Google Forms, quantitative)
  • Interview participants: 6 — 1 chronic asthma patient, 2 doctors, 1 caregiver, 1 physiotherapist — plus 1 field observation
  • Survey respondents: 13 chronic asthma patients
  • Focus: Symptom recognition, activity monitoring, inhaler reliance, caregiver support, clinical visibility, emotional impact

Screener

  • Interviews: chronic asthma patients, treating doctors, caregivers, and a physiotherapist directly involved in day-to-day asthma care
  • Asthma patient interviewee: managing the condition for 15+ years — the most lived expertise in the research set
  • Caregiver role: actively administers treatment and responds during symptom episodes
  • Survey: individuals currently managing chronic asthma, across a range of severity and experience

What We Asked

Question 1

Walk us through how you decide when to stop during physical activity, and what you do after.

Question 2

On a scale of 1–5, how confident do you feel while doing heavy physical activities?

Question 3

If a system could help you understand how much activity is safe for you each day, how useful would that be?

Key Finding 1

Stops are decided by physical signals alone — no tracking, just long-term personal experience — followed by inhaler use.

Key Finding 2

Rating 3: 6/13 · Rating 5: 4/13 · Rating 1: 2/13 — most patients manage exertion through constant low-grade uncertainty.

Key Finding 3

Very/Extremely useful: 10/13 (77%) — strong, unprompted value for the product.
